Which GST data will be used?The required tax payment variables will be picked from GSTR-3B (ITC Utilisation (sum of IGST+CSGT+SGST+CESS), tax paid in cash(sum of IGST+CSGT+SGST+CESS)).Data should be taken for all GSTINs belonging to the Company PAN.The GSTIN-level values should be added together to get the company-level tax payment.3. What are the three things shown in the chart?ITC UtilisedShows the amount of Input Tax Credit used by the company towards its tax liability.This is shown as the green portion of the bar.Cash Tax PaidShows the amount of tax that the company actually paid through cash.This is shown as the blue portion of the bar.Total Tax PaymentsShows the company's total tax payment for that period.It is represented by the dotted line.Total Tax Payments should represent the combined amount of ITC Utilised + Cash Tax Paid, based on the GSTR-3B values being used.4. How will company-level calculation work?First identify the Company PAN.Find all GSTINs linked to that PAN.Take the required GSTR-3B tax payment data for every GSTIN.Add the values of all GSTINs together.The final result will represent the total tax payment of the company.5. How will the half-year calculation work?The financial year will be divided into two periods:H1: April to SeptemberH2: October to MarchFor each half-year:Add the ITC Utilised values.Add the Cash Tax Paid values.Calculate/derive Total Tax Payments.Show the result as one bar.6. What will the user see?X-axis: Half-year periodY-axis: Amount in ₹ Cr.Green: ITC UtilisedBlue: Cash Tax PaidDotted line: Total Tax PaymentsThis allows the user to understand not only how much tax was paid, but also how the company paid it — through ITC or cash.7. Last 1 Year / 3 Years / 5 YearsThere should be a selector in the corner:1 Year | 3 Years | 5 Years1 Year: Show half-yearly data.3 Years: Show half-yearly data.5 Years: Show annual data on the main chart so that the chart does not become crowded.The detailed half-yearly data should still be available when the user drills down.8. Percentage change on hoverWhen the user moves over a bar, the tooltip should show:ITC UtilisedCash Tax PaidTotal Tax PaymentsPrevious period's Total Tax Payments% change in Total Tax Payments, itc utilization, cash paymentOn hovering over any bar, show the percentage change from the previous half-year.Calculation:Percentage Change = (Current Half-Year Turnover − Previous Half-Year Turnover) / Previous Half-Year Turnover × 100Positive percentage = turnover increased.Negative percentage = turnover decreased.First available period should not show an artificial 0% change because there is no previous period.Year to date Percentage Change = (Current Year Turnover − Previous Year Turnover) / Previous Year Turnover × 100Month to monthPercentage Change = (Current month Turnover – Previous month Turnover) / Previous month Turnover × 100This tells the user whether the company's total tax payment has increased or decreased compared with the previous period.9. Bar click / drill-downWhen the user clicks on a particular bar, the system should open the details for that selected period.The first level should show the GSTIN-wise contribution.This will allow the user to understand which GST registrations contributed to the company's total tax payment.10. Missing GST dataBefore calculating the company-level value, the system should check whether GSTR-3B data is available for all GSTINs under the PAN.If data is missing for any GSTIN, a small pop-up should appear.The pop-up should show:GSTIN numberPeriod for which data is missingMessage that the tax payment value may be incomplete.The user should get two options:Fetch Data | Continue Anyway11. Which GST data will be used?The data will be picked from GSTR-1.The system should check the filing status of GSTR-1 for each GSTIN under the Company PAN.The filing behaviour should then be combined at the company level.3. What does each donut show?Each donut represents one financial year.For every financial year, there are normally 12 monthly GSTR-1 returns.The 12 months are divided into:On-Time FilingDelayed FilingNon-FilingThe centre of the donut should show:12 MonthsThe user can therefore quickly understand how the company performed during that financial year.4. On-Time FilingCount the number of months where GSTR-1 was filed on or before the required filing date.Show the number of months.Also show the percentage out of the total 12 months.5. Delayed FilingCount the number of months where GSTR-1 was filed after the required filing date.Show the number of delayed months.Also show the percentage out of the total months.6. Non-FilingCount the number of months where the required GSTR-1 was not filed.Show the number of non-filing months.Also show the percentage.7. Company-level calculationFirst identify the Company PAN.Find all GSTINs linked to that PAN.Check GSTR-1 filing status for each GSTIN and each month.Combine the filing information to create the company-level filing behaviour.8. Important point for multiple GSTINsThe system should not simply add the filing percentages of different GSTINs.The backend should first determine the filing status at the GSTIN-month level and then apply the company-level aggregation logic.The business rule for how multiple GSTINs are represented for the same month should remain consistent.9. Percentage calculationFor each financial year:On-Time % = Sum of On-Time Months across all GSTINs ÷ Sum of Total Applicable Months across all GSTINs × 100Delayed % = Sum of Delayed Months across all GSTINs ÷ Sum of Total Applicable Months across all GSTINs × 100Non-Filing % = Sum of Non-Filing Months across all GSTINs ÷ Sum of Total Applicable Months across all GSTINs × 100The three categories should together represent the company's filing status for the period.10. Financial years shownThe chart can show:FY 2021–22FY 2022–23FY 2023–24The system should use the same logic for any future financial year.11. Year-wise comparisonThe user should be able to compare the company's filing behaviour across years.The chart should make it easy to see:Whether on-time filing is improving or reducing.Whether delayed filing is increasing or reducing.Whether non-filing is increasing or reducing.12. Hover requirementWhen the user moves over any section of the donut, show:Filing categoryNumber of monthsPercentageFinancial yearFor example, the user should immediately understand how many months were delayed and what percentage that represents.14. Click / drill-downWhen the user clicks a section or a financial year:Open the detailed filing information for that year.Show the relevant GSTIN-wise filing status.The user should be able to identify which GSTINs contributed to:On-time filingDelayed filingNon-filing15. Missing data handlingThe backend should check whether GSTR-1 filing information is available for all relevant GSTINs and months.If data is missing, a small pop-up should appear.The pop-up should show:GSTINMonthFinancial yearMissing data statusThe user should get:Fetch Data | Continue Anyway16. If user clicks "Fetch Data"Redirect the user to Operational Status / Data Fetching.Show the GSTINs/months where data is missing.Allow the missing GSTR-1 data to be fetched.Once data is available, the filing chart should be updated.17. If user clicks "Continue Anyway"Continue generating the chart using the available data.Missing information should remain NULL.It should not automatically be treated as Non-Filing.The chart should indicate that the result is based on incomplete data.18. Important differenceOn-Time = GSTR-1 was filed on time.Delayed = GSTR-1 was filed, but after the due date.Non-Filing = GSTR-1 was not filed.NULL = Filing information is unavailable in the system.NULL must not be treated as Non-Filing.GVA(Gross value added) TrendGross Value Added (GVA) Trend1. What is the chart for?This chart shows three important things about the company's business activity:Total PurchasesTotal RevenueGross Value Added (GVA)It helps the user understand:How much the company is purchasing.How much revenue it is generating.How much value the business is creating after considering purchases.2. Which GST data will be used?Purchase-related data will be taken from GSTR-2B.Revenue-related data will be taken from GSTR-3B.The data should be collected for all GSTINs linked to the Company PAN.The GSTIN-level data should then be combined to create the company-level view.3. Total Purchases  Total Purchases (Company) = Σ Taxable Value of all Suppliers across all GSTINs under PANThis shows the company's total purchase value for the selected period.Purchase information will be taken from GSTR-2B.The result is shown as the blue bars.4. Total RevenueTotal revenue (Company) = Σ Taxable turnover Value across all GSTINs under PANThis shows the company's total taxable revenue/turnover for the selected period.Revenue information will be taken from GSTR-3B.Data from all GSTINs under the Primary PAN should be combined.The result is shown as the green bars.5. Gross Value Added (GVA)GVA shows the value created by the business after considering its purchases.For this chart, GVA can be calculated using:GVA = Total Revenue − Total PurchasesGVA is shown as the yellow line.The backend should calculate GVA only after the purchase and revenue values have been aggregated at the company level.6. GVA MarginThe chart can also show GVA Margin in the tooltip.It tells the user how much of the company's revenue remains as value added after considering purchases.GVA Margin = GVA ÷ Total Revenue × 1007. Company-level calculationThe calculation should happen at the Primary PAN level:Primary Company PAN↓Find all linked GSTINs↓Fetch GSTR-2B purchase data + GSTR-3B revenue data↓Aggregate GSTIN-wise data↓Create company-level Purchase and Revenue↓Calculate GVA↓Calculate GVA Margin↓Display chart8. Half-yearly calculationThe current chart is showing half-yearly data.H1: April to SeptemberH2: October to MarchFor every half-year:Add the relevant purchase values from GSTR-2B.Add the relevant revenue values from GSTR-3B.Calculate GVA.Calculate GVA Margin.9. What will the user see?Blue bars: Total PurchasesGreen bars: Total RevenueYellow line: Gross Value AddedLeft Y-axis: Purchase and RevenueRight Y-axis: GVAX-axis: Financial half-year10. Percentage changeWhen the user moves over a particular half-year, the tooltip should show:Total Purchases% change in Purchases from previous half-yearTotal Revenue% change in Revenue from previous half-yearGVA% change in GVA from previous half-yearGVA MarginChange in GVA MarginThis allows the user to understand not just the value but whether the business is improving or weakening.11. GVA Margin changeFor GVA Margin, the change should ideally be shown in percentage points (pp) rather than percentage change.For example:Current GVA Margin: 33%Previous GVA Margin: 31%Show:+2 ppThis is easier for the user to understand.12. 1 Year / 3 Years / 5 YearsThere should be a time-period selector in the corner.1 Year: Show half-yearly data.3 Years: Show half-yearly data.5 Years: Show annual data to avoid making the chart crowded.The underlying half-yearly data should still remain available for drill-down.13. Click / Drill-downWhen the user clicks on a particular period:Open the details for that selected period.Show the GSTIN-wise purchase and revenue contribution.The user should be able to understand which GSTIN contributed to the company's purchase and revenue.Recommended drill-down:Company → Financial Year → Half-Year → GSTIN → Monthly Data14. Missing data handlingThe system should check whether required data is available for all GSTINs.If GSTR-2B or GSTR-3B data is missing for any GSTIN:Show a small pop-up.Show the GSTIN for which data is missing.Show the period for which it is missing.Give two options:Fetch Data | Continue Anyway15. If user clicks "Fetch Data"Redirect to Operational Status / Data Fetching.Show the GSTIN and missing data source.Allow the missing GSTR-2B/GSTR-3B data to be fetched.Once the data is available, the chart should be updated.16. If user clicks "Continue Anyway"Continue showing the chart using available data.Missing values should remain NULL.Missing data must not be treated as zero.The system should indicate that the displayed result may be incomplete.17. Important difference0 = Actual reported value is zero.NULL = Data is missing/unavailable.NULL must not be converted to 0.This is particularly important for GVA because if purchase or revenue data is missing, the system should not create a false GVA value.18. Simple backend requirementThe backend needs to:Take the Primary Company PAN.Find all GSTINs linked to the PAN.Fetch purchase data from GSTR-2B.Fetch revenue data from GSTR-3B.Aggregate the data across all GSTINs.Create H1/H2 totals.Calculate GVA = Revenue − Purchases.Calculate GVA Margin = GVA / Revenue × 100.Calculate period-on-period changes.Provide data for 1Y, 3Y and 5Y views.Provide GSTIN-wise data for drill-down.Handle missing data through the Fetch Data / Continue Anyway flow.19. In very simple wordsThis chart compares how much the company purchases with how much revenue it generates and shows the value created by the business after purchases. Purchase data comes from GSTR-2B, revenue data comes from GSTR-3B, and all GSTINs under the Primary PAN are combined to create the company-level view.
